SITE SURVEY CHECKLIST

Everything to gather before an installer visits, so your quotation is accurate the first time.

Published October 2026 · By the CNE Zone team · All articles

A site survey is the walkthrough where an installer studies your building before recommending any equipment. The more prepared you are, the more accurate the quotation will be, and the fewer surprises appear once work begins. Use this checklist to prepare.

1. Know what you want to protect

Make a short list of your priorities: people, cash, stock, equipment, data, or all of the above. Mention incidents you have already experienced, such as theft, unauthorised entry or disputes that footage could have settled. A clear picture of the risk helps the installer propose the right level of protection instead of a generic package.

2. Gather building information

  • Approximate area and number of floors.
  • Floor plans, even rough sketches, if available.
  • Number of entrances, gates, windows and stairwells.
  • Building materials, such as concrete, glass or false ceilings, which affect cable routes and wireless coverage.

3. Identify key areas

List the spots you care about most: main entrance, reception, cash counter, server room, store, parking, loading bay, perimeter wall. Note which of these should have cameras, door control, alarms or all three.

4. Check existing infrastructure

  • Are there existing cameras, recorders or alarm panels? Do they work?
  • Is there structured cabling, and where do the cables end?
  • Where would a rack or recorder be placed, and is the space ventilated?
  • Is there a dedicated power supply, and do you have a UPS or generator?

5. Consider internet and network needs

If you want to view cameras remotely, mention your internet provider, connection speed and whether you have a static address or are open to a secure remote-access method. Tell the installer who currently manages your IT, as they may need to coordinate on network settings.

6. Decide who needs access

For access control, estimate how many people will use the system, whether visitors and contractors need temporary access, and whether you want attendance reports. List the doors and which groups of people should be allowed through each.

7. Think about retention and viewing

Decide how many days of footage you want to keep and who should be able to view or export it. This directly affects storage size and cost. Also consider whether a central monitoring desk, a manager's phone or a simple local screen is enough.

8. Set a realistic timeline and budget range

Tell the installer whether the work is urgent, phased or part of a larger renovation. Sharing a budget range is not a weakness; it allows the installer to suggest the best value options and show where a phased approach can help.

9. Questions to ask during the survey

  • What will the system cover and what will it not?
  • What equipment is proposed, and why?
  • What is included in the price: cabling, installation, configuration, training, documentation?
  • What warranty and support is offered, and how are faults reported?

10. After the survey

You should receive a written proposal with equipment lists, camera or device positions, a timeline and clear pricing. Compare proposals on scope as well as price, since the cheapest quote often leaves out cabling, storage or commissioning.
